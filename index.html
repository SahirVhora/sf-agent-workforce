Why not create agents as employees?

It is the obvious shortcut and it breaks four things at once.

  • Headcount and statutory reporting. Agents inflate FTE, gender pay gap denominators and workforce analytics.
  • Payroll replication. EC Payroll and third-party payroll integrations pick up employment records. An agent with a job record is a payroll defect waiting to happen.
  • Licensing. Many HCM licences count employee records.
  • Meaning. Contract, notice and leave fields fill with junk.

The model instead

Agents get their own object that references the same foundation data, positions and people as employees, so they show up in org, cost and capacity views without polluting employment data.

Questions

Should AI agents be created as employees in SuccessFactors?

No. Creating agents as users with job records inflates headcount and statutory reporting, risks payroll replication, can consume per-employee licences and fills employment fields with meaningless data. Model them as their own effective-dated MDF object that references positions, cost centres and accountable people.

When do the EU AI Act high-risk rules apply to HR systems?

From 2 December 2027 for stand-alone Annex III systems, including employment and workers' management, under Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. Prohibitions have applied since 2 February 2025 and Article 50 transparency duties since 2 August 2026.

Does the EU AI Act apply to UK employers?

It can. Article 2 brings providers and deployers outside the EU into scope where they place AI systems on the EU market or where the system's output is used in the EU, for example an HR system used for EU-based workers or candidates.

Which HR uses of AI are high-risk?

Annex III point 4 covers recruitment and selection (targeted job adverts, filtering applications, evaluating candidates) and decisions affecting terms of work, promotion, termination, task allocation based on individual behaviour or traits, and monitoring or evaluating performance and behaviour.
